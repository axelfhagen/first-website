# Trip to Mt. Fuji
*September 8th 2025*

A breathtaking journey to Japan's most iconic mountain. Standing at 3,776 meters, Mount Fuji offered an unforgettable experience with stunning sunrise views.

Originally I had booked a mountain hut lodging for September 5th. Due to a typhoon, the mountain was closed the planned day and I was panicking as the season for climbing Mt. Fuji. closes at the 10th, and the lodgings already sold out in May.

I researched everything I could, staying in a temporary hostel in Tokyo. I found (read: claude research found) a mountain hut for accomodating two people on the 7th station on the Yoshida Trail. There was a single room left and I booked it, even though I was going alone.

I realised I could save some money by finding a travelcompanion to share the lodging-costs with.  I posted on Reddit, in various Discord channels and even made a Hostelworld event (by accident, I kind of meant to just send out a message in a Tokyo group...). The night before departure, Rafaela contacted me through Hostelworld, saying she would be interested in joining me. Rafaela was a 30-year-old woman from Brazil who had just landed in Tokyo, jetlagged, from Qatar that same evening.

I met Rafaela for the first time in the bus stop the morning the day of the climb. She had booked a woman-only bus and so it was a short "Hello nice to meet you, cool you could join" before we spent the next 2 hours or so apart on separate buses before stopping in Fujikawaguchiko. Here we waited for approximately 40 minutes for the next bus that would take us to the Yoshida 5th Station Trailhead. We stocked up on water, chocolate and onigiris from 7-Eleven. Rafaela told me about life in Brazil, which sounded unbelievable to me. I told her about life in Norway, which sounded unbelievable to her.

The climb was challenging but incredibly rewarding. The view from the summit at dawn was absolutely magical, with clouds rolling below and the first light painting the sky in brilliant colors.
